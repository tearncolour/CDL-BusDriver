## 0.17.7

**A native-cab bus draws the housing of its card reader** (the BYD B6 session's report of 13 November, `Reports/BYD_B6_2025/20261113-cab-corrections/BusDriver-CardReader-改造.md`: only the reader's lit screen, scanner window and lamp hung in the air by the door). Local build; not published; not installed; the installed 0.17.6 is the base. Offline only: nothing was run in the game.

- **Cause** (the report's, checked on this tree). The mod builds its card reader's housing into the runtime `Cab` part. With `"cabRender": "native"` the `Cab` part is one of those the game asset stands in for, hidden once the native cab is there; and the native cab drops the asset's own triangles inside a reader's housing box, since the mod draws the reader. So neither drew the housing. The reader's lights are objects of their own (`BusCabSystem.Readers`) and stayed.
- **Fix** (the report's three changes, ported by hand: its tree is the 0.12.23 line, not this one). A native-cab model with readers gets one more static part, `CardReaders`, after its mesh's parts, under the shell, and the housing is built there instead of in `Cab` (`BusInterior.CardReaderPart`); in a part of its own the far LODs keep it (248, 24 and 24 triangles for the B6's one reader). The hand straps' swing ends at the mesh's last part, so the added part does not swing. A runtime-cab model, and a native-cab model without readers, have the part table they had.
- **Two things done differently from the report.** The strap range is counted from the posed model, not read from the mesh: the report's version made posing a profile load its mesh, which threw for a profile that has none (this tree's assembly test caught it). And the in-game probe's strap check (`BusVehicleProbe`) ends at the mesh's last part too.
- **Checks.** 16 new (`ReaderPartTests`): a runtime-cab pack keeps its readers in `Cab` and its part table; on the installed B6 0.1.4 pack one part is added under the shell, the housing is there at three LODs inside the reader's box about the profile's reader point, `Cab` is smaller by exactly the housing at LOD0 and LOD1 (one housing, never two) and no other part changed, with the replaced parts hidden the reader part is shown and still while every strap swings, and the native cab's own part lists do not reach it. The report's own check program (`CabCorrectionCheck.cs`) run on this tree as the test command `b6-cab-check`: passes. Five mutations caught (the housing left in `Cab`, the reader swinging with the straps, a reader part for a runtime cab, the housing built twice, the far LOD losing it). The suite 3,451 checks with the same 5 failures as before.
- **Not verified**: nothing in the game. To look at in the B6 from the driver's seat and from the door: the housing behind the screen and the scanner window, by day and by night, the reader's lamp at a tap, the door leaves through their whole travel, the three LODs from outside, and that there is one housing (the asset's own reader, if the pack models one, is dropped by its box as before). A native-cab pack with several readers was not tried: none is installed.

## 0.17.5

**The driven bus's glass and native cab are no longer made in one frame, and a bus that keeps failing is no longer rebuilt without end** (the owner's audit of 0.17.3.2 and 0.17.4, 10 November: the glass prepared in 183 ms of one main-thread update for the SWB6109EV12G and 152 ms for the ZK6128H, outside the 2 ms budget; any update exception still released and rebuilt the whole bus on the next frame, the path of the 9 November out-of-memory crash; "应该从根本修复问题"). Four smaller findings of the same audit are fixed too (below). Local build; not published; not installed; 0.17.4 (install23) is the base. Offline only: nothing was run in the game.

- **The glass and the native cab read the game's geometry themselves, off the main thread.** Both took the meshes with `RenderPrefab.ObtainMeshes`, which reads, decompresses and uploads the whole model on the calling frame to give a few glass submeshes (the "obtain" share was 142 to 889 ms in earlier logs). Now `Systems/NativeGeometryFile.cs` reads the game's geometry format (version 1, as `GeometryAssetSave` writes it: zstd and meshopt blocks, attribute-major), Unity's asynchronous file reads load it (polled, never waited for), and a worker thread decodes it and cuts the wanted submeshes into pieces with only the vertices they use (`NativeGeometryRead`). The main thread makes the materials and the pane meshes, one at a time within the frame's 2 ms (`BusGlass.Step`, `BusNativeCab.Step`); the game's own `GeometryAsset` is not touched (its load state, its reference count, its meshes). The vertex layout is the one the game gives Unity (`GeometryAsset.CreateMeshes`: the header's formats and streams, packed normals and tangents byte for byte, four `UInt8` as one `UInt32`). A pane copied the whole source vertex buffer before; now it holds only its own vertices, with 16-bit indices when they fit.
- **Prepared ahead means prepared.** Ahead of driving, every pack bus's glass is read and cut once for the session (one at a time); the take-over then makes only the panes. The model upload of the take-over also keeps to the budget (it finished in one frame when the bus was taken while its upload was still running, and a model built for the drive was uploaded at once), and so does the riders' model. The bus the player selects goes first: its background model build is moved to the front of the queue (`CabPrebuild.Promote`), its glass and upload before the others. Until a section is complete the game's own body stays, as the 0.16 rule says.
- **A failure is held to where it happened.** The signs, the cluster, the mirrors, the headlights, the riders, the card readers' lights, the LED screen, the wrap, the door sounds, the indicators, the driveline link, the pad's rumble, the ride's motion and the sign overlay each fail on their own: that part is released and tried again after 1, 2, 4 and 8 s, then left off until another bus is taken over; the bus goes on. A failure of the bus itself releases it as before, but the same vehicle model is taken over again only after the same pauses, and after five failures in a row the game's own bus stays for the session (`Systems/FailureGate.cs`; a bus drawn complete for 10 s counts as working again). The rebuild storm of 9 November (a failure every frame for 107 s, 112 rebuilds) is at most 5 builds.
- **Wrap pictures over 4096 x 4096 are refused on every path.** The worker's decoder refused them, but the picture then went to the game's `LoadImage` on the main thread, which has no limit: an 8192 x 4096 PNG of 127 KiB passes the zip checks and decodes to 128 MiB. The size is now read from the header (PNG IHDR, JPEG frame header) before any decoding, for the wrap, its thumbnail and the main-thread path; a refused picture keeps the wrap worn, with the reason in the log.
- **Changing to another bus of the same line restarts the announcements.** The source the announcements follow now includes the vehicle (`AnnounceSource`): another bus, even heading for the same stop, drops the old queue and announces its next stop and approach (before, the state of the old bus stayed and nothing was said).
- **Forcing the doors shut or ending the shift releases the bus in a new city.** The forced departure was `now - 1800` in unsigned frames: in the first 1800 frames of a city it wrapped round to a frame about four thousand million away, which the game's boarding waited for. `BusShiftRules.ForcedDeparture` gives frame 1 there (the game cannot treat any departure as overdue before frame 1801; the bus leaves once its boarding passengers are on) and the old value after; held to a copy of the game's own test (`TransportCarAISystem.StopBoarding`).
- **The packer takes every profile version the game runs.** The packer read versions 1 to 3, the profile reader 1 to 4 (the ZK6128H's profile is 4): both now ask `VehicleProfileJson.Supported` and `IsAssembly`. The old test that expected the packer to refuse a version 4 assembly encoded the fault; it now expects a version above the reader's to be refused.
- **The passenger activity check says what differs.** `VerifyNativeBoarding` reported "native passenger activity settings differ" (the LCK5120A's game check of 10 November); it now lists every field that differs with the expected and the actual value. The LCK5120A's own difference is not fixed here: the next game check will say what it is.
- **Checks.** 69 new: the geometry format against files written as the game writes them (stored and compressed with the game's own PipelinePlugin), the layout of plain and skinned meshes, refused corrupt headers, the cut (groups, dropped triangles, piece limits, remapped indices that give back every corner), and every geometry file of the seven installed packs (34 files, 7,155,369 triangles) decoded against the vertex ranges and bounds their exporter wrote beside them (worst difference 0.104 mm); the gate (pauses, giving up, the storm), the queue's promotion, the announcement switch with the audit's scenario, the departure against the game's test, the pack versions, the picture limit (PNG, JPEG, overflow, files with no readable size). Mutations caught: no giving up, the old departure, the vehicle left out of the announcement source, no picture limit, the wrong stream of a skinned mesh, the first mesh's offsets for every mesh, indices not remapped, the packer back to version 3. The suite 3,419 checks with the same 5 failures as before.
- **Not verified**: nothing of this ran in the game. To look at: the log lines "Bus glass for ...: ...; read and cut off the main thread in N ms, P pane(s)" and "Native cab for ...: ...", the 60 s "Bus interior" line's worst frame at a take-over, that the glass and the native cab look as before (the same triangles in other pane meshes; the decoder is checked against the packs' own data, the Unity side only by the build), and the doors of a section whose glass is made after the body was already shown.

## 0.17.4

**The stop announcements play while the player rides a bus as a passenger** (the owner, 10 November, choosing it after 0.17.1 gave the ride the next-stop screen), **and the driver's feet of another session are merged** (below). Local build; not published; the installed build (that session's 0.17.3.2) is the base. Offline only: nothing was heard in the game.

- **Before**, the announcements (`BusAnnouncementSystem`) followed the shift alone: a ridden bus was silent.
- **Now** they follow the shift of the player's bus or, without one, the bus the player rides (`BusRide`, read from the game four times a second by the ride system): "Next stop X" when the bus is under way after its target moved on (and, as for a shift, once a moment after the ride begins), "Approaching X" within 150 m by the road, "X" when it stands at the stop with its doors open (the game's Boarding flag and a stopped bus), the terminus and the line as the shift says them (TransitTimetables first, else the line's last stop). The line's sentences are made ahead in the background when a ride starts, from the stop the bus heads for, as for a shift (`BusRide.StopNames`, filled with the route).
- **What a ride does not have.** "Not stopping at X": the game does not tell which stops a bus passes without stopping. The depot leg: whether the bus came out of the depot is not known, so a first stop that is the terminus is announced as the final stop. The option that turns the announcements off turns them off for a ride too.
- **When an announcement is due** is now `Systems/AnnounceRules.cs` (the facts of a frame, the state between frames, what is due), the same rules for a shift and a ride. The shift's announcements are unchanged: the old code is kept in the test as an oracle and gives the same announcements over 24,000 random frames of states, targets, distances and skips (620 announcements). A ride is checked over a whole stop (next stop, approaching, the stop at the doors, the next stop after it, each once), the target moving on with the doors still open, a ride that begins at a stop with its doors open, a route lost and found again.
- **The option text** ("Stop announcements") says they play while riding, without the "not stopping" one.
- **Checks.** 10 new (`AnnounceTests`); three mutations caught (the next stop said with the doors open, the arrival said every frame the doors are open, a ridden bus taken as always under way). The suite 3,350 checks with the same 5 failures as before.
- **Not verified**: that the voice plays in a ride, that the stop named at the doors is the right one (it follows the bus's target, which the game keeps on the boarded stop: read from the game's code, not heard). The tree also holds an edit of `Systems/BusNativeCab.cs` by another session (9 November 16:48, the native cab keeps moving door overlays and the wrap faces the runtime owns), which is in the installed build already; it is not described here.
- **Merged: the driver's feet of the BYD B6 session** (installed by it on 10 November 01:46 as 0.17.3.2, dll d556c5a6, from `out/b6-driver-feet-production-001/source` on this tree; the owner, 10 November: "合并脚部功能后做 0.17.4"). An optional `cab.driverFeet` (a left and a right foot frame: origin, rotation, knee pole) in a pack's profile puts the procedural driver's soles on the pedals (`Interior/Models/DriverFootPose.cs`, `BusRiders`, `VehicleProfile.CabData`, `VehicleProfileJson`, `BusCabSystem.Riders`); a profile without it is as before. The five files are that session's, copied whole: the four shared ones were built on this tree's own versions, which had not changed since, so nothing of this tree was dropped. Without the merge, installing 0.17.4 would have taken the feet out of the installed game. Checked: that session's own test (`Tests/DriverFeet.Tests`, run from a copy against this tree with its recorded baseline) passes its 140 checks, the 111 part/LOD fingerprints of the legacy driver unchanged, and the anchored driver's geometry is the same file as in its run; this tree's suite gives the same 3,350 checks and 5 failures. Its own changelog entry is that session's to write.

## 0.17.3

**A bus that is not one of ours no longer vanishes in the chase view, and the driver view can be moved up, down, left and right** (the owner, 9 November: "我开第三方公交车怎么跟随视角下车没了" with a screenshot of the chase view of a street with no bus in it; and "支持调整驾驶室视角高低和左右"). - **The vanished bus: cause (read off the logs of that run, 14:30 to 14:31).** For a bus that is not one of ours (a workshop or game bus: "not one of our buses, our cab inside only, its own body outside") Bus Driver draws its cab model inside and asks the driving mod to hide the game body in the driver view. The driving mod does that, and shows the body again when the player goes to the chase view (its log: "game body shown" at 14:30:38.001 and at 14:31:14.619). The body watch (`BusCabSystem.HideWatch.cs`, since 0.15.4, written for the ridden bus whose closed native door showed in the doorway) puts `Hidden` back wherever it is lost, and its test of whether the body should be hidden asked only whether the camera was fixed on the outside, never whether the view was the driver view: so in the chase view it took the body away again, 4 ms after the driving mod had shown it (14:30:38.005, "Hidden was gone ... put back") and in the same millisecond the second time. The same code ran for our own buses, but their body is not drawn in the chase view anyway (our model is), so only a bus of someone else showed it. The watch counted its own re-hiding as the game removing `Hidden`, which is what the log lines say.
- **The vanished bus: fix.** The decision is `NativeBodyRules.ShouldBeHidden`: viewing, and the whole body asked hidden (we draw the whole bus: every view), or the cab body asked hidden and the camera is in the driver view on a seat (`Core.CabViewActive`, false in the chase view and with a camera fixed on the outside, which Core documents: the body is shown there). `CameraMounted` and the Core wrapper of it (`MountedCameraActive`) were only used by the old test and are out.
- **Driver view up/down and left/right (options Cab view: "Driver view: eye height" and "Driver view: eye left and right").** Two sliders, centimetres, 25 each way, one step 1 cm, default 0 (no change; a settings file from before them has 0). Up and right are positive, whichever side the driver sits on (Unity model space: +Y, +X). The offset is added to the camera eye where it is handed to the driving mod (`BusCabSystem`, `CabViewRules.Offset`; the number is held to the range): the model eye (`BusInterior.Eye`, what the mirrors and the probes are set up from) is not changed. It applies at once, in the driver view of a shift and of a ride. The mirrors are drawn from the eye the camera has, so they show what they would for the moved eye.
- **The range was measured, not guessed.** At the nine corners of the range the camera is at least 10.4 cm from every solid surface of the model (the generic bus 25 cm down: the dashboard; SWB6710EV01G 25 cm up: a pole, 15.1 cm; SWB6109EV12G 26.9 cm at the nearest, the steering wheel); the seat itself is 20, 33 and 44 cm. The test guards 8 cm. That is the model, not the camera: the near clip plane the driving mod uses (0.04 m in its log) was not tried against it in the game.
- **Checks.** 31 new (the numbers and the directions on both traffic sides, the range and a hand-edited number, the offset is on the camera eye and not on the formal one, texts in both languages, the clearances; the watch rule in all 16 combinations, and the case of the report); mutations caught: left and right reversed, the range not held, the old test of the watch. The suite 3,340 checks with the same 5 failures as before.
- **Not verified.** Nothing in the game: that the third-party bus now stays in the chase view (the log lines to look for are `Hidden was gone` at the moment the chase view starts, which should no longer appear), how the sliders feel and whether the eye at the edge of the range looks right in each pack. The sliders are in the options page; there is no key to move the eye while sitting in the view.

## 0.17.2

**A vehicle pack without destination signs no longer makes the bus rebuild itself until the game runs out of memory** (the owner game crashed on 9 November at 13:24 with 0.17.0, `Could not allocate memory`; diagnosed by the ZK6128H session in `ModelSource/ZK6128H/performance-v208/DRIVING-PERFORMANCE.md`, whose patch is merged here). Local build; not published; the installed 0.17.1 is the base. Offline only: nothing was run in the game.

- **Cause (from the logs, not seen again in the game).** The ZK6128H pack declares `signs: {}`, which the profile reader accepts: no front, side or rear sign (matrix 0, size 0). The cab system built its outside signs for it all the same: `NewSign` to `NewHd` to `HdSign` to `LedMesh` threw `ArgumentException: A screen needs dots, a size and an ink`. `OnUpdate` catches any exception and calls `ReleaseBus`, so the next frame measured and built the whole native cab and its glass again: 112 times from 13:22:21 to 13:24:08 on one entity (median 0.7 s apart), until a 128 MiB native allocation failed with 4.08 of 4.30 GB in use. The exception is logged once in 600 updates, so one log line is not one failure. A pack with side or rear signs but no front one had a milder form: a missing front sign was also the test for "not built yet", so its signs were destroyed and made again every frame.
- **Fix** (the other session patch, `no-sign-fix/no-signs.patch`; this file is its candidate byte for byte): a sign the profile does not declare (matrix 0) is not built, `NewSign` gives null, as `BusAiSignSystem` already did for the AI buses (`Matrix.x > 0`); the sign set has a built flag of its own (`m_SignsCreated`) instead of the front sign being non-null; it is made again only when a sign that exists is destroyed, when the style changes or when the bus is released. No model name anywhere. The extra side signs cannot be null: the reader asks them 8 columns or more.
- **Checked.** The other session harness (`Tools/ZK6128H/SignLifecycleCheck`: the real `UpdateOutside`, `NewSign` and `ReleaseSigns`, fake graphics and fonts) was run again from a copy: the patched methods pass its 8 cases (no signs in both styles over 600 updates, side and rear without a front sign, a lost renderer, release and re-enter, the built-in bus with its three signs, a printed board with extra side signs, the font still loading); the original fails with the same exception as in the log. The methods it tests are, whitespace aside, the ones in this tree (compared by script, with the pre-patch methods as a control that differs). The suite 3,309 checks, the same 5 failures as before; the official build compiles the change.
- **Not done, and not verified.** No frame time, memory trend or two-minute drive of the pack in the game (the other session asks for them). **The storm is still there for any other exception**: `OnUpdate` releases the whole bus on every exception and builds it again the next frame, without a pause or a limit; this fix removes the one cause that was found. A back-off (a growing pause after repeated failures, then a stop) would bound it; it was not made, because it changes when a failing bus is tried again. The pack own findings in that document (an 818,038-triangle static native cab, mirrors, door colours) are the pack side.

## 0.17.0

**A second kind of door linkage, the parallel four-bar** (the owner, 9 November: "给BusDriver加入新的开门连杆系统", from `Tools/ZK6128H/BUSDRIVER-MOTION-INTERFACE.md`). Local build; not published; the installed 0.16.21 is the base and this contains 0.16.22 (below). Offline only: no pack uses it yet and nothing was run in the game.

- **The mechanism.** A drive arm and a balance arm of equal length turn on two fixed shafts by the same angle and carry the door leaf at two joints that make a parallelogram with the shafts: the leaf moves along the arc of the arms and does not turn at all (the roller kind turns it). It is general, not a model: no branch for any vehicle, the numbers come from the pack's profile. The hinged doors and the roller-in-a-rail doors are unchanged: every check they had passes as before.
- **Contract: `cdlb-vehicle` version 4.** `doors[].linkage[]` may carry `kind`: `parallel_four_bar` with `shaft`, `joint`, `balanceShaft`, `balanceJoint` (closed positions, `[x, z]`), `shaftDegrees`, `drivePart` and `balanceDrivePart` and no `guide`; `guide_rail` names the old kind, and a linkage without a kind is the old kind. Everything is refused with the door, leaf and field: a `kind` in a profile below version 4, an unknown kind, a `guide` on a four-bar, a balance field on a roller, a missing field, one drive part used twice, drive parts that are not one run after the 34 contract parts, and numbers that are not a parallelogram (`J - S = K - B` within 1 mm), shafts less than 2 cm apart, an arm under 3 cm, a turn outside 5..170 degrees, a value that is not finite. ## 0.12.2

Second performance round: the wrap's load off the main thread, the packs' meshes read faster, and in a better order (the user, 19 October: "start the second round of performance optimisation of the CDL mods, mainly Core and BusDriver").  **Built and tested offline only: not deployed, not run in the game.**
- **What the logs of 18 October showed (0.11.2, the road probe).** The block table's `wrap skins` had a worst frame of 761 ms and 776 ms (one frame each, the two times a wrap was selected: the log lines "Body wrap ... left.png: see-through pixels ..." come 90 to 240 ms apart on the main thread), and the first frame of a drive had `eye` 674.6 ms. Everything else of the cab was 0.14 ms a frame; the guidance 0.3 to 0.54 ms a frame.
- **A wrap loads on a worker thread.** Selecting a wrap (or the zip changing) read the zip, decoded five PNGs with the game's `LoadImage`, and ran the outline and colour work (`WrapPackage.Prepare`: two distance transforms over every picture) all in one frame. Offline the colour work alone is 250 ms a wrap on a fast machine (about 140 ms now), about three times that on the game's Mono. Now a background thread of below-normal priority reads the zip, decodes the pictures and prepares them; the main thread makes one texture a frame from the finished pixels and shows the wrap when all of them are there. **The wrap worn stays until the new one replaces it** (before, the freeze came first and the swap was instant; now the swap is instant and there is no freeze). A load that fails (a wrong model or layout, a damaged zip) removes the old wrap and warns, as before. Another vehicle's wrap is unloaded at once. The log has a new line per load: `Body wrap "X": pictures made on a background thread in N ms, uploaded in M ms over K frame(s) on the main thread.`
- **`PngDecoder`, a PNG decoder for any thread** (`LoadImage` only runs on the main thread). It takes what wraps are: not interlaced, 8 bits (grey, grey + alpha, RGB, RGBA) or 1 to 8 bits (indexed, grey), with tRNS, up to 4096 x 4096. Everything else (16 bits, interlaced, a JPG, a damaged file, an invalid chunk checksum) is left to the game's `LoadImage` on the main thread exactly as before, so no wrap that worked stops working. **Checked against two independent references:** WPF's own PNG decoder on the 10 pictures of the two real wraps in the repository root (0 differing pixels in 14.1 million), and the counts the game logged for the same wraps on 18 October ("13666 outlined white, 688274 recoloured", "5586 / 843978", "12127 / 358413" and the other seven pairs): all ten pairs reproduced exactly with the model's own windows and doors (`CDLC.BusDriver.Tests wrapreal`). 30 checks in `PngDecoderTests` (every colour type and depth, the five row filters, split chunks, tRNS, the refusals, 3000 randomly damaged files).
- **The colour work is cache-friendly** (`WrapPackage.OutlineAreas`, `Decontaminate`): the column passes read a picture one pixel per row down a column, a new cache line each time; they now take 16 columns together. The same arithmetic per column: 4 checks against the old code kept as `WrapReference` (random pictures of every awkward width, wrap-sized pictures, the real wraps) give the same pixels and counts bit for bit. Five pictures of a wrap: 253 -> 138 ms on this machine.
- **The textures are written from the pixels as they are** (`Texture2D.SetPixelData`, with the old `SetPixels32` as the fallback if it throws) instead of filling a `Color32` array one pixel at a time. **Not seen in the game.**
- **The packs' meshes are read in big steps.** `VehicleMesh.Load` asked the gzip stream for four bytes at a time (25 million times for the SWB6109EV12G's 1.77 million triangles, 123 MB). It now fills a 128 KB buffer. A hash of every triangle read (swatch, positions, normals, as bits) is the same before and after for all three packs (`meshbench`). The mesh load on this machine: K18 393 -> 120 ms, SWB6109EV12G 1757 -> 437 ms, SWB6710EV01G 773 -> 113 ms. (The `eye` block of 674 ms in the first frame of a drive is the main thread waiting for this load: the mesh is a `Lazy` that the first caller loads and the others wait for.)
- **The preparation reads every pack's mesh first**, then builds the models one after another (before: each mesh was read at the start of its own model's build, behind the other buses' builds of 1 to 10 s), so the first drive of any bus is less likely to find its mesh unread.
- **The riders' figures are made over several frames at a take-over** (`Fill`: a budget of 3 ms a frame, the next frame carries on while anything changed). A game citizen's figure costs 1 to 2 ms (two compute buffers, property blocks, dozens of objects); a full bus's 60 to 100 of them in the one frame of the take-over was the worst frame of the cab's `riders` block in the log of 18 October (202.6 ms). Boarding at a stop is unchanged (one figure at a time). Not measured in the game: how long the bus takes to fill at a take-over (about 1 to 3 figures a frame, so a few tenths of a second).
- **Dev tools:** `CDLC.BusDriver.Tests wrapbench | wrapreal | buildbench | meshbench | columnsbench` (timings and the real-wrap counts; see `WrapBench.cs`). Offline model builds on this machine: K18 front 434 ms first / 167 steady, SWB6109EV12G 1703 / 473, SWB6710EV01G 605 / 161 (the game logged 1112 ms for the K18 front and 10281 ms for the SWB6109EV12G on the background thread on 19 October).
- Tests: 2766 checks (34 new). **Not checked in the game:** that `SetPixelData` writes level 0 of a texture with a mip chain as `SetPixels32` did; the look of the wrap after a swap (it must be the same picture); the new log line's numbers on the Mono runtime; selecting wraps quickly one after another; a wrap selected while driving (the old one stays for about a second).

## 0.9.11

No more "Duplicate prefab ID" at load for our buses (the user's request, 17 October: 20 such warnings a boot, one per prefab of the four packs)
- **Cause.** The game's package writer (`PackageHelper.ResavePrefabAssets`) calls `MarkCurrentPrefabObsolete()` on every prefab it packs, and the packed prefab keeps its GUID, so the "obsolete" identity it adds equals the current one. At load `PrefabSystem.AddPrefab` registers the prefab and then finds that alias already registered: `Duplicate prefab ID: CarPrefab:SWB6109EV12G (...)`, the same for every mesh prefab, the K18's trailer and its meshes. Editor-made assets from Paradox Mods show the same line for the same reason; other mods' `RenderPrefab:... (hash)` lines without a source are real duplicate registrations of theirs and not touched here.
- **Fix.** The package export (`BusPackageExport`) now takes a prefab's own identity (same type, name and asset hash as the entry's `.cid`) out of its `ObsoleteIdentifiers` before the `.cok` is published. The prefab text is Odin Serializer's dialect, not JSON (an object may hold values without a name, a Vector3 is `{"$type": "...", 0, 0, 0}`; `"$type": "N|type"` defines a type number, `"$iref:N"` refers to an object), so a small reader of that dialect (`OdinPrefabText`) finds the entries and the text is changed by cutting and splicing: the entry goes with its separator, `$rlength` follows, a type definition the entry carried moves to the next entry using it, an entry anything refers to is refused, and every other byte stays. The archive is written by the export itself (every entry Stored; the untouched entries' local headers and data byte for byte; the central directory with its offsets changed), not by `ZipArchive`: on .NET Framework, the game's runtime, `ZipArchive` writes Deflate even for "no compression", and the game reads Stored only (the first pack run with this fix failed on exactly that; the tests, on .NET 10, had not seen it). Real aliases (SWB6109EV12G's eight earlier GUIDs) stay in their order. Every other entry is verified byte-identical; the export of an export is byte-identical. The empty-cache removal stays as before for the strictly checked model (`Fixes.All`); the other models get the identity fix alone (`Fixes.SelfIdentifiers`) instead of a plain copy.
- The packs have to be made again for this to show (`pack_asset.sh`); nothing changes at run time.
- Tests (2458 checks): the identity fix on hand-made prefab texts (own entry alone, among real aliases, first with the type definition, other hash/name/type/asset, no identifiers, a mesh prefab, `$iref` refused, the dialect's unnamed values and escapes), in a package (entry rewritten with time stamp and attributes, `.cid` and other entries untouched, idempotent, each fix alone, a prefab without its `.cid` refused), and the real `Asset/*.cok` fixtures (one own identity per prefab taken out, exported again byte-identical).

## 0.9.1

Two things found in the first run of 0.9.0 in the game (the user's report: no fine vibration, a higher frame cost)
- **The DualSense's fine haptics were muted by this mod's own rumble reports.** The pad has two exclusive vibration modes, rumble emulation and native audio haptics (the ones the driving mod plays through the pad's audio endpoint), and the flags of the last HID output report choose: a report with the rumble flags keeps the pad in emulation. The report was built with the flags on every write, motors at zero included, and the first write came when driving started. So the audio haptics were ignored, while the engine and road rumble had been left to them (`PadHapticsActive`): nothing at all was felt. Now, while the driving mod's audio haptics own the actuators, a report carries the rumble flags only while a motor really rumbles (a door, a control's click); when it ends, one report says "motors off" with the flags and the next one has none, which hands the actuators back to the audio; the same on leaving the bus or unloading the mod. Without the driving mod's audio haptics (a pad on Bluetooth, the option off) the flags go on every report as before. Tests: 2013 checks (the idle, trigger-only, door-rumble, stop and own-rumble reports). **Not yet checked on the pad itself.** Sources for the two modes: the dse-haptics notes and SDL's DualSense driver (it sets "disable audio haptics" with a rumble and clears it when the rumble ends).
- **The articulated joint cost 2.9 ms a frame in the game's profiler** (`BusArticulationSystem`, render preparation; the target is under 1 ms for the whole system). Three causes, all in the joint's per-frame path, fixed:
  - *The bend itself (the main one).* The K18's nearest joint LOD has 14 244 vertices, and the old code bent each one on its own with two spherical interpolations (one of them a call into Unity's native `Quaternion.Slerp`), rebuilt the whole mesh from two arrays and recalculated its bounds, every frame the pose changed in any digit. Now `JointDeformer` (pure, `Interior/Models`) bends the distinct (position, normal, weight) vertices once (a joint mesh is a triangle soup: most vertices repeat) with one rotation matrix per weight (17 weights in LOD0), copies them to every place they stand in the mesh in the mesh's own order and count (the probe reads vertex i back and compares it with source vertex i), and the mesh takes position and normal as one vertex-buffer upload with fixed bounds that are set once (`radius + MaxSeparation`), not recalculated. **Measured offline on the real K18 joint (this machine's .NET, not the game's slower Mono):** a bend of LOD0 0.723 ms before, 0.047 ms now (15x); LOD1 0.325 to 0.023 ms; LOD2 0.251 to 0.019 ms; every vertex and normal equals the per-vertex result to under 2e-5 m. The old code also paid for Unity's native slerp per vertex, so the game's old figure is higher than the reference timed here.
  - *The scan.* A moving camera asked for a new scan every frame (a move of 0.1 m or a turn of 0.1 degrees), and each scan asked `VehiclePacks.ForPrefab`, which builds a string key per call, for every vehicle with a layout in the city. A driving camera always moves. Now the pack profile of each prefab is remembered, and a moving camera asks for a scan at most every 0.1 s (the periodic scan is every 0.5 s and its radius allows for that travel).
  - *Bending again for nothing.* A pose that differs from the one the mesh was bent for by less than 0.2 mm at the joint's reach (a relative-rotation component within 2.5e-5, an offset within 0.1 mm) is not bent again; both endpoint rings stay within that of their sections (the probe allows 2 mm). A moving bus changes the pose every frame, so this only saves the standing and straight-rolling cases. A joint at 35 m or 80 m no longer changes its LOD, and so its mesh and materials, every frame as the distance wobbles (a 3 m margin: `VehicleArticulation.LodFor`).
  - Nothing about which joints are drawn changes. Tests: 2039 checks (the deformer against the per-vertex reference on a synthetic bellows and on the K18's three LODs, rotated front sections, the endpoints, normals, the radius bound, equal and unequal vertices, bad input, the LOD margin including a wobble around 35 m, and the timing). **Not measured in the game:** the system's new frame cost, nor how the mesh upload and the draw behave there (offline there is no Unity mesh). The log now says it: `Articulation: X ms a frame over N frames (joint scans, native poses, bend and upload)` every 30 s (six at most) while a joint is drawn, plus profiler markers `CDLC.Articulation.Scan/Poses/Joints`. The bus as one of the city's bus models

Passengers on board (option "Passengers on board")
- The passengers and the driver are the game's own citizens (option "Game citizens as passengers", on by default): each passenger looks as the game made that resident (style, clothes, hair, face, skin), or a citizen of their age and gender in summer or winter clothes by the temperature; the game's meshes and materials drawn through its GPU skinning with bone and meta buffers of the mod's own (never the game's global buffers), posed by the mod from the game's animation clips: seated from the bench-sitting loop (four variants), standing from the idle loop (three), holding a strap and the driver at the wheel with an arm IK on top (the game has no clips for either). Standing passengers hold the straps or the rails and lean a little with the bus.
